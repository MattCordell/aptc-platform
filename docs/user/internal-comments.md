# Internal comments

Internal comments let Reviewers and Administrators leave notes for each other on a submission or
on a catalogue entry. This page explains where the **Internal comments** panel is, who can see
it, how you post and withdraw a comment, and what the page does when the platform refuses. You
must be signed in as a Reviewer or an Administrator to see the panel.

## Who can see the comments

Only Reviewers and Administrators can read internal comments. The submitter cannot, and neither
can a Member, an Observer or a visitor who is not signed in. For them the panel is not on the
page, and the page does not hint that it exists.

The platform decides who may read and who may write, from the permissions you hold. A page that
was open when your role changed can show a panel that the platform now refuses. The panel then
shows the platform's own sentence.

## Find the panel

The panel is in the main column, below the content:

- On a submission, open it from the Submissions page. See
  [Reading a submission, registering interest and withdrawing](reading-a-submission.md). The panel
  sits below what the submitter sent.
- On an entry, open it from the catalogue. See [Reading an entry](reading-an-entry.md). The panel
  sits below the terms and the code bindings.

A draft entry has no public page, so its comments cannot be reached from these screens yet.

## Read the comments

The comments run from the oldest to the newest. Each one shows who wrote it, their username, the
date, and the text. If nobody has commented, the panel says so.

The text shows exactly as it was typed. Line breaks are kept. A comment that contains symbols
such as `<` or `&` shows those symbols. The page never treats a comment as formatting, so it
cannot change how the page looks.

## Post a comment

1. Write your comment in **Add an internal comment**. It holds up to 2,000 characters.
2. Choose **Post comment**.

The comment appears at the bottom of the list, the box empties, and a screen reader announces
"Comment posted." A comment cannot be edited or deleted after you post it. You can withdraw it.

The platform refuses a blank comment, and spaces alone do not count. The box then says
"Enter a comment." If the text holds a character that cannot be shown, such as a zero-width
space, the box shows the platform's explanation and keeps your text.

## Withdraw a comment

Withdraw a comment when it is wrong or no longer applies. Any Reviewer or Administrator can
withdraw any comment, not only their own.

1. Choose **Withdraw** under the comment.
2. Write a reason. It is required, spaces alone do not count, and it holds up to 2,000
   characters.
3. Choose **Confirm withdrawal**.

The comment stays on the page with its text. It now carries a **Withdrawn** label, and it shows
who withdrew it, the date, and the reason. Everyone who can read internal comments sees that
reason, so write it for the next reviewer. A withdrawn comment has no **Withdraw** button.

Choose **Cancel** to close the box without withdrawing the comment.

## When the platform refuses

The box stays open or keeps your text, and the page shows why:

- **The comment was already withdrawn.** Another reviewer withdrew it first. The page reads the
  list again, so you see the withdrawal. **Confirm withdrawal** stops working. Choose
  **Cancel**.
- **You may not do this.** The platform's own sentence appears. This happens when your
  permissions changed after you opened the page.
- **You have not accepted the current terms of use.** Accept the terms when the page asks, then
  try again.
- **The comments could not be loaded.** The panel says so and offers **Try again**. If the
  platform refused you, it shows its own sentence and no **Try again**, because a second attempt
  cannot succeed.

If you cannot tell why something is refused, ask an Administrator.

## Using it with a keyboard or screen reader

Press Tab to move through the panel: each **Withdraw** button, the comment box, then
**Post comment**. Each **Withdraw** button names the author, the date and the start of its comment, so a
screen reader can tell them apart. The panel has its own heading, **Internal comments**. A
screen reader announces "Comment posted." and "Comment withdrawn." After you withdraw a comment,
focus moves to that comment. Status is always written in words as well as shown in colour.
